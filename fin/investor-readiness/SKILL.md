# Investor Readiness Assessment

Systematic assessment and preparation of businesses for capital raising. This skill evaluates readiness across seven dimensions and produces a prioritised action plan to close gaps before approaching investors.

## Readiness Dimensions

Score each dimension 1-5 (1 = not ready, 5 = investor-grade):

1. **Financial reporting** — are financials audited, timely, and IFRS-compliant? Is there a credible 3-year forecast?
2. **Governance** — independent board members? Proper minutes? Conflict-of-interest policies?
3. **Legal structure** — clean cap table? No unresolved shareholder disputes? IP properly assigned?
4. **Market position** — can you articulate TAM/SAM/SOM? Do you have evidence of competitive advantage?
5. **Team** — does the management team have the skills to execute the growth plan? Key person gaps?
6. **Systems** — is there an ERP/accounting system producing reliable data? CRM with pipeline visibility?
7. **Track record** — revenue growth trend? Customer retention? Unit economics proving out?

## Minimum Viable Readiness

Before approaching any institutional investor, these must be in place:

- Audited or reviewed financials for the last 2-3 years
- Board of directors with at least one independent non-executive
- Shareholders' agreement (or company MOI with equivalent protections)
- Clean Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) records
- Tax compliance certificate (or evidence of SARS good standing)
- B-BBEE certificate or exemption (for SA businesses)
- Financial model with clearly stated assumptions

## Valuation Benchmarking

Use comparable transactions, not aspirational multiples:

- **SA mid-market** — typically 3-6x EBITDA for profitable businesses
- **Tech/SaaS** — 5-12x ARR depending on growth rate and retention
- **Agriculture** — 1-3x revenue or 6-10x EBITDA depending on asset base
- **Early stage** — pre-revenue valuations based on comparable funding rounds, not DCF

Always present a range, not a single number. Investors respect founders who understand their own uncertainty.

## Gotchas

1. **Premature fundraising** — most businesses approach investors 6-12 months too early. Every gap the investor finds reduces valuation and trust. Invest in readiness before investing in pitch decks.
2. **Cap table complexity** — messy cap tables (dormant shareholders, unresolved options, undocumented loans) are deal-killers. Clean the cap table before any investor engagement.
3. **Founder salary expectations** — investors expect founders to be modestly compensated during the growth phase. If the financial model shows founder salaries above market rate, it signals misaligned incentives.
4. **B-BBEE in SA** — for any business targeting government or corporate clients in SA, B-BBEE compliance is not optional. Investors will assess the B-BBEE structure and its sustainability post-investment.
