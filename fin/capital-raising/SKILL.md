# Capital Raising

End-to-end capital raise workflow for AI agents advising businesses seeking growth capital, acquisition funding, or project finance. Grounded in real deal experience across African markets.

## Key Capabilities

- Capital structure design: equity vs. debt vs. hybrid instruments, dilution modelling, covenant analysis
- Funding document preparation: business plans, information memoranda, investor pitch decks, term sheets
- Financial model for fundraising: 3-5 year projections, unit economics, use of funds waterfall, returns analysis
- Investor targeting: PE/VC mapping, DFI eligibility, impact investor criteria, family office approach
- Process management: raise timeline, data room preparation, Q&A tracking, closing mechanics
- Valuation support: pre-money/post-money, comparable transactions, DCF cross-check

## Capital Raise Process

A well-run capital raise follows this sequence:

1. **Preparation (4-6 weeks)** — financial model, business plan, data room, legal structure review
2. **Marketing (2-4 weeks)** — investor longlist, teaser distribution, NDA management, management presentations
3. **Due diligence (4-8 weeks)** — financial, legal, commercial, technical DD; Q&A management
4. **Negotiation (2-4 weeks)** — term sheet, shareholder agreement, conditions precedent
5. **Closing (2-4 weeks)** — CP satisfaction, funds flow, board reconstitution

Total timeline: 3-6 months for a well-prepared business. Underprepared businesses take 9-12+ months and often fail.

## Funding Document Standards

Every funding document must answer five investor questions within the first two pages:

1. **What** — what does the business do, in one sentence?
2. **Why now** — what market timing or catalyst makes this the right moment?
3. **How much** — quantum sought and instrument type
4. **Returns** — what return profile and timeline can the investor expect?
5. **Team** — who is running this and why should the investor trust them?

## Gotchas

1. **Valuation anchoring** — founders always overvalue; investors always undervalue. Use comparable transactions as the anchor, not DCF. DCF is a cross-check, not a negotiation tool.
2. **Use of funds** — "working capital" is not an acceptable use of funds line. Be specific: X for inventory, Y for hires, Z for equipment. Vague use of funds signals poor planning.
3. **Data room hygiene** — incomplete data rooms kill deals. Before approaching investors: audited financials (or reviewed if early stage), company registration documents, material contracts, IP ownership evidence, director CVs.
4. **African PE landscape** — deal sizes under $5M are too small for most PE funds and too large for most angel networks. This "missing middle" requires creative structuring — revenue-based financing, mezzanine, or syndicated angel rounds.
