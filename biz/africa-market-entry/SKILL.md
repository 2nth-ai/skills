# Africa Market Entry Strategy

Strategic advisory for AI agents supporting businesses entering African markets. Built from on-the-ground deal-making experience connecting funders, investors, and projects across the continent.

## Key Capabilities

- Market entry modes: direct investment, JV, franchise, distribution agreement, licensing
- Regulatory navigation: exchange control (SARB), competition authorities, sector-specific regulators
- Local partner identification: due diligence, JV structuring, agent agreement frameworks
- Country risk assessment: political stability, currency risk, legal enforceability, infrastructure gaps
- Cross-border deal flow: identifying opportunities, structuring transactions, managing multi-jurisdiction complexity
- Cultural navigation: business practices, negotiation styles, relationship-building across African markets

## Market Entry Decision Framework

For any African market entry, assess:

1. **Market attractiveness** — GDP growth, sector size, competition intensity, regulatory openness
2. **Entry barriers** — local ownership requirements, licensing, import duties, forex restrictions
3. **Partner necessity** — can you operate independently or do you need a local partner? (Most sectors: yes)
4. **Capital requirements** — setup costs, working capital cycle, repatriation restrictions
5. **Risk tolerance** — political risk, currency volatility, contract enforceability, corruption exposure

## Key Markets

| Market | Entry complexity | Opportunity | Watch out for |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|---------------|
| South Africa | Medium | Largest, most sophisticated | Load shedding, BEE requirements |
| Kenya | Medium-Low | East Africa hub, tech-forward | Land ownership restrictions |
| Nigeria | High | Largest population, massive TAM | Forex access, security, corruption |
| Ghana | Low-Medium | Stable, mining/agriculture | Small market, utility reliability |
| Egypt | Medium | Manufacturing, large population | Military economy, bureaucracy |
| Rwanda | Low | Ease of doing business leader | Tiny market, authoritarian governance |

## Gotchas

1. **"Africa" is not a market** — it's 54 countries with different legal systems, currencies, languages, and business cultures. Never generalise. A strategy that works in Kenya will fail in Nigeria.
2. **Relationship-first** — in most African markets, the relationship precedes the transaction. Budget 3-6 months of relationship-building before expecting commercial outcomes. Cold outreach has near-zero conversion.
3. **Forex risk** — many African currencies are volatile or restricted. Structure contracts in hard currency where possible, or build hedging costs into pricing. Naira and cedi exposure has destroyed margins for many entrants.
4. **Contract enforcement** — legal systems vary widely. In some jurisdictions, court processes take 3-5 years. Build enforcement mechanisms into deal structure (escrow, offshore arbitration clauses) rather than relying on local courts.
5. **Corruption** — it exists. Have a zero-tolerance policy and document it. The costs of compliance are always lower than the costs of getting caught. UK Bribery Act and US FCPA have extraterritorial reach.
